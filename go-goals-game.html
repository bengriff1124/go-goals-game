Play & build the future

A digital adaptation of the UN board game about the 17 Sustainable Development Goals — now played as competing nations racing the same deadline from very different starting lines. Each team draws a random nation that quietly sets how fast they move and which goals come easy. Every correct answer secretly builds toward the real behaviors the world needs to achieve each goal — hidden until 2030, when a final round plays out and the results are revealed all at once.

Official rules
  1. Set up 2–4 teams and how many players are on each. Every team shares one token and draws a random nation — permanent for the whole game, and shown openly all game long.
  2. A nation sets two things: how fast it moves (double speed, normal, or half speed) and which of the 17 goals it already has the infrastructure for.
  3. There are 63 spaces on the board, numbered in one continuous path from Start to 2030 — follow the arrowed guide line to see which way the path turns. Teams take turns rolling a single die and moving forward that many spaces, multiplied by their nation's speed.
  4. You cannot roll past a numbered SDG space (1–17) — if your move would carry you over one, you stop there first and answer it before continuing.
  5. Talk it over as a team, then pick an answer together — whoever's holding the device taps it in.
  6. If your nation already has what a goal needs, one correct answer is enough. If it doesn't, you must answer two questions correctly in a row — missing either ends your turn.
  7. What you're actually building is hidden: every correct answer quietly adopts one real behavior a society needs for that goal, credited to your team. None of this is shown until the game ends.
  8. Playing a Persona? Your question becomes an open-ended challenge written for that character instead — type an idea, huddle with your team, see a fact, and continue (no privilege gate on these).
  9. Land on a purple Persona space → draw a new character for your team to play until your next one. Amber Consequence spaces hit your team with something they didn't choose. Blue Speak Up spaces let you argue on behalf of a rival team's character — get it right and you roll again.
  10. Reaching 2030 doesn't win the game — it's a deadline. The first team to arrive triggers one final turn for every other team, then the results are revealed: which goals the world actually achieved, and which team's nation made that easy or hard.

Rules, artwork inspiration and question content adapted from the official “Go Goals!” board game created by the United Nations Regional Information Centre (UNRIC) with artist Yacine Aït Kaci (YAK) — go-goals.org. This is an unofficial fan-made digital version for educational use; not affiliated with or endorsed by the UN.
