`goal-01.json` cite only these. Nothing here is written from memory.

> **Note on the headline number.** Sources give 808M (`SDGR25`), 826M (`UN1`) and 831M
> (`WB`) — different vintages of the same $3.00/day measure. Questions therefore ask for
> **order of magnitude** ("about 800 million") rather than false precision. This is also
> better pedagogy: the point is 1-in-10, not a memorised digit.

## Facts

### The measure itself
1. The International Poverty Line is **$3.00 per person per day**, in 2021 purchasing power parity dollars. `WB` `OWID` `UN1`
2. PPP means the line is adjusted for inflation *and* for price differences between countries, so it buys the same amount everywhere. `OWID`
3. The measure counts non-monetary income too — food a family grows for itself is included. `OWID`
4. Living below it means not affording "a tiny space to live, some minimum heating capacity, and food that would not induce malnutrition." `OWID`

### How many, and where
5. About **800–830 million** people live in extreme poverty — roughly **1 in 10** people. `SDGR25` (808M, 9.9%, 2025) `UN1` (826M) `WB` (831M)
6. In **1990** about **2.3 billion** people were in extreme poverty — roughly **44%** of the world. `OWID` `WB`
7. **1.5 billion** people escaped poverty between 1990 and 2022 on the $3/day line. `SDGR25`
8. That averages ~**42.9 million per year**, about **118,000 people every day**, over 35 years. `OWID`
9. In **1820**, about **three-quarters** of the world lived in extreme poverty. `OWID`
10. The global rate first fell below **50% in 1956**; to 25% by 2001; to ~12.5% by 2014. `OWID`
11. **Sub-Saharan Africa** is the poorest region — nearly **40%** of its population is in extreme poverty. `OWID`
12. Extreme poverty is increasingly concentrated in Sub-Saharan Africa and in **conflict-affected and fragile** places. `WB`
13. About **65% of the world's poor (740 million)** live in **middle-income** countries, not low-income ones. `MPI25`

### Progress has stalled
14. COVID-19 pushed roughly **50 million** more people into extreme poverty around 2020 — the first substantial rise in a generation. `OWID`
15. If current trends hold, about **9%** of the world will still be in extreme poverty in **2030**. `UN1`
16. Global poverty reduction has "slowed substantially" — economic sluggishness, debt, the pandemic, conflict and weather shocks. `WB`
17. Aid targeted at poverty reduction has fallen back to **2015 levels**. `UN1`

### Work doesn't guarantee escape
18. In 2024, **6.9%** of employed people — over **240 million workers** — lived below $2.15/day. It was 8.4% in 2015. `SDGR25`
19. `UN1` states it differently: **284 million workers (7.9%** of the global workforce) in extreme poverty in 2025, down from 11% in 2015.
20. Working poverty by group (2024): **Least Developed Countries 30%**, Landlocked Developing Countries 20%, Small Island Developing States 9.5%. `SDGR25`

### Social protection
21. In 2023, **52.4%** of people worldwide were covered by at least one social protection benefit — the first time over half. It was 42.8% in 2015. `SDGR25` `UN1`
22. **3.8 billion people** still have no social protection at all. `SDGR25` `UN1`
23. **1.4 billion children** have no social protection. `UN1`
24. Coverage by income group (2023): high-income **85.9%**, upper-middle **71.2%**, low-income **9.7%**. `SDGR25`
25. A gender gap persists: **50.1% of women** vs **54.6% of men** covered (2023). `SDGR25`
26. An extra **$1.4 trillion a year** would fund basic social security in low- and middle-income countries — **3.3%** of their combined 2024 GDP. `UN1`

### Children
27. About **412 million children** under 18 live in households below $3/day — roughly **1 in 5** children. `WB`
28. Children are over-represented: over **half** of the 1.1 billion multidimensionally poor are children. `MPI25`

### Poverty is more than income
29. **1.1 billion people** across 109 countries are **multidimensionally poor** — about **17%** of the 6.3 billion surveyed. `MPI25`
30. The MPI measures **3 dimensions** via **10 indicators**: health (nutrition, child mortality), education (years of schooling, attendance), living standards (cooking fuel, sanitation, drinking water, electricity, housing, assets). `MPI25`
31. Common deprivations: lack of clean cooking fuel, housing, sanitation, nutrition, electricity. `MPI25`
32. Land rights (2022): **66%** feel their tenure is secure, but only **43%** hold official documents — and only **24% of women** do. In Sub-Saharan Africa just **15%** have formal documents. `SDGR25`

### Climate and poverty overlap
33. **887 million** poor people live in areas facing at least one climate hazard; **309 million** face three or four at once. `MPI25`

### Money and policy
34. Governments spend very differently on essential services (2023): advanced economies **60%**, emerging and developing economies **43%**. `SDGR25`
35. Goal 1's targets: **1.1** eradicate extreme poverty; **1.2** halve poverty in all its dimensions; **1.3** universal social protection; **1.4** equal economic rights and property access; **1.5** resilience to climate and economic shocks. `UN1`
